# rat-kingdom strategic review: why it can't be daily-driven, and what stands between it and an environment-aware harness

*2026-08-16. Sources: full codebase survey of rat-kingdom (101,476 lines of Rust in crates/) and imp-castle (22,932 lines of Go, 13,433 non-test), the docs/ failure-history corpus, git log, and operator memory of recurring gotchas. All code-level claims below were independently fact-checked against the tree; line numbers are as of HEAD c2b9839. Feature sizes in §3 are `wc -l` over the named files (production = lines before the first `#[cfg(test)]`).*

## Executive summary

rk's day-to-day flakiness comes from a small set of seams — seven, catalogued below — where the system parks work and waits. They are not one grand architectural mistake; they fall into three honest classes: **deliberate safety defaults that were never revisited** (respawn and runaway detection exist in code but ship disabled), **ordinary bugs** (the scheduler drops failed minutes; a panicked workflow instance stays `Running` forever; nothing kills a rat's process after `rk done`), and **two genuinely structural escalation dead-ends** (steward review timeouts and failed instances terminate in a `need`/inbox row that nothing is guaranteed to read). What unifies them is the consequence, not the cause: every failure path ends at a passive surface — the inbox — and **the inbox has no reliable reader.** imp-castle had one: the King, an LLM in a tmux pane that the daemon poked every 10 seconds, plus a human who could always attach. rk correctly burned down imp's keystroke-injection transport, but the escalation channel it kept still assumes a mind is watching it, and rk shipped neither a standing coordinator nor a notification story to replace that mind. The "poke the driving agent" ritual you perform is you being that reader by hand.

Three pieces of good news. First, most of the fix is cheap: config flips plus three or four small bug fixes convert the majority of the seams; only the escalation dead-ends need new machinery. Second, the ingress half of your production-awareness goal is **already substantially built** — `rk ingest`, signed per-source auth, the five SDLC signal kinds, dedup receipts, semantic transition detection, and built-in CI/alert reactions exist and are tested, further along than `docs/2026-08-04` admits. Third, the orthogonal mass (factory analytics/dashboard, product-to-code, ballots, and onboarding's scale — roughly 20–25k lines) is cleanly separable: nothing on the core loop depends on it.

On sequencing, this report's first draft argued "fix the substrate entirely before touching reactivity." Review of that draft convinced me it was wrong, by its own cost estimates: the smallest reactive slice (a webhook shim feeding the existing `rk ingest`, plus a per-repo policy opt-in) costs days and is read-only, so **run it early as a forcing function** — let real alerts reveal which seams actually bite, and grow the recovery machinery against observed failures instead of speculation. What must come first is only the cheap tier: recovery defaults on, the small bug fixes, and a redeploy story.

## 1. The seam catalogue

Everything here parks until a human or driving agent issues the next `rk` command. Classes: **[D]**efault never revisited, **[B]**ug, **[S]**tructural dead-end.

| # | Seam | Mechanism | Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Crashed/orphaned rat** | `respawn_enabled: false`, `burn_usd_per_min: 0.0` (`config.rs:420-435`). Workflow `wait` holds open for orphaned agents until step timeout; no `rk workflow retry` exists. | D |
| 2 | **Steward review timeout** | No verdict in 15m → landing entry escalates a `need`, is marked processed, and is dedup-blocked from redelivery for that `(repo, branch, head_sha)` (`landing.rs:577-874`). Human-only recovery. | S |
| 3 | **Scheduler failed dispatch** | Cursor saved unconditionally even when dispatch errored (`scheduler.rs:131-142`) — the minute is dropped; a wedged `Running` instance makes its schedule skip forever (single-flight, no staleness bound). The reactor got this right (`reactor.rs:279-281` pins on retryable failure); the scheduler didn't. | B |
| 4 | **Hung workflow instance** | A panic in an instance's execution future skips `finalize`; the instance stays `Running` forever. Nine background loops (`server.rs:545-882`); none sweeps instances. `await_fleet_capacity` polls forever (`workflow_exec.rs:3447-3456`). | B + S |
| 5 | **Orphaned in-progress ticket** | Drain refills only from `status == "open"` (`tickets.rs:425-436`); an errored rat deliberately leaves its ticket `in_progress` (`supervisor.rs:2727-2747`) and no sweep ever reopens it. | B |
| 6 | **Daemon restart kills the fleet** | Children are `kill_on_drop`; restart orphans every rat. Compounding: auto-start execs `current_exe()` with no version comparison (`client.rs:261-282`; version is exposed at `server.rs:5551` but nothing checks it), so merged daemon features silently don't exist until a manual restart — which itself costs the fleet. | S (restart semantics) + B (no version check) |
| 7 | **Post-completion runaway burn** | Nothing kills the harness process at `rk done` (kill sites are budget-stop, sweep-escalation, dismiss only); the sweep filters `is_live()` and Completed isn't live, so even enabled runaway detection can't fire post-completion. | B |

Threads the docs corpus names itself: each new mechanism re-earns crash-safety by hand (landing and drain got durable cursors + idempotency; the scheduler didn't); completion is a convention (`rk done` tuple) over a turn protocol with no native "task finished" — the root of the TKT-160/173/175 "green but not done" lineage; safety invariants have lived in hand-copied unversioned files (TKT-171: 5 of 108 lands silently dropped by a stale `~/.rat-kingdom/workflows/steward.cue`, all caught by humans); and state is split across `agents.json`, the tuplespace, cursor files, instance JSON, and git. The TKT-171 postmortem's own line is the thesis in miniature: *"The evidence was in the tuplespace the entire time; nothing read it."*

One more structural fragility that recurs rather than parks: **identity-by-name.** TKT-136/146 — your "workflow-spawned rats signal-killed ~1s in" gotcha — was a fresh rat matching a predecessor's 2-day-old `harness_result` and being SIGTERMed by the trailing dismiss; it has been fixed twice on different keys (spawn-time reservation, then archive exclusion + `floor_at` bounds). Names are durable identity keys stamped into tuples, logs, and branches; any consumer that joins on name alone can time-travel. This needs its own remedy (§5, S3), not just vigilance.

## 2. The imp-castle lesson

imp's entire control channel is a 185-line, zero-test `tmux.go`: `send-keys` + hardcoded 500ms sleep + Enter; readiness = regexing captured panes for a trailing `>`; liveness = "a tmux session with this name exists," with every tmux error mapped to "dead"; the daemon stomping `C-a C-k` into sessions it doesn't own to inject "You have new mail." rk was right to replace all of it — `rk-harness`'s "no terminal scraping, no keystroke injection, no sleeps" is a genuine, complete win, alongside real cost accounting, structured transcripts, durable state, and a crash-safe workflow engine imp never had.

But be precise about what imp's model bought, and at what bar. imp was drivable **with a human continuously present**: things broke constantly (a 210-line `respawn.go` codifies the crash ritual; the King's prompt documents the recovery playbook), and recovery was cheap because two mechanisms existed:

1. **A standing reader.** The King — orchestration policy as 140 lines of prose in an LLM session — was woken by one dumb clock (a 10-second mail poll) and exercised judgment on everything that broke. rk has no coordinator role at all (zero hits for "king" in crates/) and distributed the King's judgment across reactor, steward, drain, and inbox — mechanisms that escalate rather than judge, to a surface nothing reads.
2. **An escape hatch.** `imp attach` made every breakdown fixable by typing into the pane. rk's equivalent (herdr `--attach`) is optional and rarely used.

If the bar is *unattended convergence* — which is the bar your production-reactive goal implies — imp fails it worse than rk ever could: its liveness model can't even represent an unattended fleet. The lesson to carry forward is therefore not "bring back the King pane," but that rk deleted the reader without replacing it, and needs two distinct replacements: a **judgment-free reconciler in code** for the mechanical recoveries the King wasted tokens on, and a **deliberate operator-interaction story** for the judgment calls (§5, S2/R5). The previously-discussed-but-unbuilt "rk king" conversational dispatcher is the second half of this, and this report's earlier draft wrongly ignored it.

## 3. Orthogonal mass relative to the goal

Only ~25–30% of production code (roughly 13–15k of ~52k production lines) is on the spawn→drive→review→merge path; the rest is automation around the loop plus orthogonal subsystems. Against "harness that reacts to deployed-environment state":

| Feature | Size (prod+test) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Factory foreman** (ratatui dashboard, scorecards/recommendations, MCP facade, Python Jcode skill) | ~12,300 | **Freeze — except the typed approval boundary.** `action_approval.rs` (872 lines: propose → SHA-256 digest → approve → daemon-only execute with CAS) is exactly the gate approval-gated external actions (rollback, CI re-run) will need. Keep it *exercised* during the freeze (a smoke workflow through propose/approve/execute) so a security-critical gate doesn't rot unused. The analytics stack is read-only by its own doc, most metrics "unavailable today"; the dashboard duplicates `rk top`/`rk inbox`/`rk observe`. |
| **Product-to-code lifecycle** | ~5,700 | **Freeze.** A 10-step PM-to-implementation pipeline touching the core loop only at a terminal `workflow run`. Nothing depends on it. Planning-side ambition while the execution substrate drops work. |
| **Repo onboarding** | ~7,600 | **Keep the function, stop growing it.** It is the legitimate activation path for per-repo reaction policy — but 5 modules, 11 RPCs, and a full CAS state machine for a once-per-repo wizard is the highest complexity-per-use ratio in the tree, larger than the reactor it configures. |
| **rk-sync multi-castle replication** | ~1,700 | **Keep, no new work** (revised from "freeze" on review). If the ingest gateway ends up on a server castle — the natural home for a public webhook endpoint — while you drive from the laptop, rk-sync is precisely how that castle's alert facts and diagnosis tickets reach you, and it's validated (P6 closed). If ingest stays single-host (e.g. tailnet-only shim), it can idle. |
| **Suggest/endorse/conventions ballots** | ~600 | **Park, don't remove** (revised: the inbox open-ballot row just shipped, and conventions carry replication semantics — removal has blast radius beyond the line count). Zero conventions promoted over 277 spawns; a social mechanism for a fleet size that doesn't exist yet. Revisit if the fleet ever runs unattended long enough to generate suggestions. |
| **Pheromone decay / resolution trails** | ~200 | Cheap to keep; another undriven conceptual surface. |
| **Dead steward CUE + drifted trigger entries** | — | **Delete** (already ticketed, TKT-01M048…). Two review/gate implementations in-tree, one dead, both tested — the drift pattern behind TKT-171. |
| **Harness adapters** (claude, codex, axe, jcode, fake) | each <550 | **Keep claude + fake unconditionally; audit the rest by usage.** Every adapter is maintenance on the critical path when its harness's protocol shifts. The tier-routing rationale justifies two production adapters, not four. |

Explicitly keep: rk-ledger budgets (486 lines — the bounded-reactions gate on every dispatch), tickets/drain/inbox, reactor, scheduler, ingest, and the workflow engine. Structural debt on the keep-side that will fight every substrate change: `server.rs` at 6,281 production lines (~85 RPC handlers + 4 background loops + ballots + onboarding + ticket-graph execution in one file) and `workflow_exec.rs` at 4,220 (hand-rolled 19-variant step interpreter).

**The meta-issue: what grew this mass.** 101k lines in under a month, substantially self-hosted, with a nightly self-improve schedule and a drain autoscaler pointed at the backlog. Factory foreman, product-to-code, and a 7.6k-line onboarding wizard are what a lightly-supervised generative loop builds. A freeze list that doesn't constrain the *generators* treats the symptom — the mass regrows, and dual-implementation drift (TKT-171's failure mode) scales with line count. Hence R6 below.

## 4. What already serves the goal — and the three real gaps

Live today: `rk ingest event|state` with per-source derived tokens and kind allowlists (source principals can only ingest — they cannot spawn or steer); a canonical `SignalEnvelope` (`ci_failed`, `ci_recovered`, `deployment_succeeded`, `production_alert_firing/resolved`); transactional `(source, delivery_id)` dedup receipts; current-state facts with semantic-digest transition detection (a `firing→firing` refresh does not re-emit); deployment-provenance correlation (`(environment, service) → artifact/revision`); built-in CI reactions that repo triggers *can* already dispatch workflows from; and a durable, idempotent reactor plus cron scheduler as the event and time axes.

Between that and "alert fires → diagnosis rat dispatched → finding lands" sit three gaps:

1. **No transport.** Nothing polls or receives from external systems — no HTTP gateway, no source manager (design slices 3 and 6, absent from crates/). A webhook must be bridged by an out-of-band shim calling `rk ingest` on the daemon host.
2. **Alerts are deliberately fused shut.** The reactor hard-excludes deployment/alert tuples from configured triggers (`reactor.rs:248-263`) — observation-only by explicit safety-ladder choice. Opening it is a policy decision, not construction.
3. **No external actions.** rk can propose but never rerun CI, roll back, or touch production (slice 7). The factory approval boundary is the ready-made contract.

**The gap the first draft missed — security of opening the valve.** Ordinary workers run with full permissions (the README explicitly rejects restricted worker modes); only onboarders get a forced read-only tool set. So the "read-only diagnosis workflow" gap 2 wants **names a rat type that does not exist yet.** And alert payload text flowing through trigger params into rat prompts is a prompt-injection channel for anyone who can influence an alert annotation. Opening alert-driven dispatch without (a) a read-only `diagnostician` role built on the onboarder enforcement machinery and (b) hygiene rules for templating external text into prompts would hand limited code-execution-adjacent capability to whoever holds an ingest token or can shape an alert. These are prerequisites of R4b, not follow-ups.

## 5. Recommendations

Revised ordering after review: the first draft's strict "all substrate, then reactivity" sequencing was contradicted by its own cost estimates. Cheap fixes and the cheap read-only reactive slice run **concurrently**; the reconciler grows incrementally behind them, driven by observed failures.

**Tier 1 — this week, config and small diffs (S = substrate, R = reactive):**

- **S1. Flip the recovery defaults and fix the small bugs.** `respawn_enabled: true` with existing caps; a runaway burn threshold; scheduler cursor pins on error (copy the reactor's pattern — one function); staleness bound on scheduler single-flight; kill the harness process group at `rk done` (or a short grace); a sweep that reopens `in_progress` tickets with no live owner; a stale-`Running`-instance timeout. Each is a small, independently shippable diff. **Every automated recovery action must be rate-capped, jittered, and initially announced** (inbox row + event tuple) rather than silently taken — the operator memory of runaway burn, spawn collisions, and stale worktrees is a catalogue of what level-triggered actors amplify. Budget the spend: respawn caps × seam frequency is new daily burn; the ledger's dispatch gate already bounds it, but set the caps deliberately.
- **S2. Fix the redeploy story — conservative half.** Version-stamp the socket handshake; on mismatch the CLI warns loudly and offers a one-command drain→restart→reconcile. Leave *automatic* self-restart behind a flag: an auto-restarting singleton daemon that execs `current_exe()` can restart-loop on stale PATH binaries (a live hazard given the mise/PATH toolchain gotcha), and drain semantics against attached herdr sessions are undefined. This closes "merged features need a restart, restart kills the fleet," which currently taxes every daemon improvement including all of the above.
- **R1. Stand up the minimal ingest bridge.** A launchd/systemd shim (or tailnet-only listener) translating one real webhook source — your alerting or CI provider — into `rk ingest event`. Days of work; read-only; exercises auth, dedup, and transition detection against reality.

**Tier 2 — next, the valve and the reader:**

- **R2. Build the `diagnostician` role, then open the alert valve per-repo.** Extend the onboarder read-only enforcement to a worker role; define payload-hygiene rules for external text entering prompts; then relax `reactor.rs:248-263` behind per-repo policy so alert transitions can fire a budget-capped, read-only diagnosis workflow whose output is a finding tuple + inbox row. This is the forcing function: it will exercise seams 1–7 under external stimulus and tell you which recoveries matter.
- **S3. Make generation-id the join key.** Names become display labels; every consumer that today joins on name (`wait`/`wait_all`, completion claims, log keying, dismiss targeting) joins on `(name, generation)` or a spawn ULID. This retires the TKT-136/146 class structurally instead of per-incident.
- **R5. Decide the operator-interaction story.** The reconciler (S4) deliberately handles only judgment-free recovery; someone still drives rk conversationally every day, and today that's an unprimed ad-hoc session you poke. Options, cheapest first: (a) a notification channel with teeth — escalations push to something you actually see (herdr notify exists; a desktop/phone push shim is small); (b) a standing operator-role session with a poll cadence — the previously-discussed "rk king" dispatcher, now justified because it only handles judgment, not recovery; (c) nothing new, if S1+S4 shrink escalations enough that a daily `rk inbox` suffices. This is a decision for you, flagged rather than made here — but it should be made explicitly, not inherited.

**Tier 3 — grown, not built:**

- **S4. The reconciler — incremental, with a written contract.** One level-triggered sweep over durable state (agents.json, tuplespace, instances, tickets, landing queue, git) asserting invariants that today have no owner: branch approved-but-unmerged → re-enqueue landing (with a fresh dedup key — seam 2's marker must become retryable); escalated `need` older than N with no ack → re-notify, don't re-park; completed rat with a live process → kill. Contract requirements, learned from this codebase's own history: a dry-run/observe mode first; an explicit arbitration rule (the reconciler is the *only* actor for terminal-state cleanup — and name which of the nine existing loops it absorbs, so the loop count goes down, not up); durable idempotency for its own actions; rate caps per invariant. Note one honest limit: "instance `Running` with no live future" is not decidable from durable state alone — that check needs an in-process heartbeat from the executor, which is why it's listed in S1 as a timeout instead. Start with two invariants (unlanded-approved branches; stale escalations) and grow it against what R2's live traffic actually breaks.
- **S5. Bind ticket-done to delivery, per delivery mode.** For `merge`/`merge-push`: done requires `merged == true`. For `push-branch`: done requires the remote branch ancestor-of-target check that `remote_branch_merged_or_gone` already implements. For `pr` mode, "merged" is a remote fact rk only learns via ingest — so this lands *after* R1, and is itself a good second ingest source (forge webhook → `rk ingest`). Closes the TKT-18/46/147 class structurally rather than by inbox visibility.
- **R6. Constrain the generators.** Freeze-listed features are not drainable: the nightly self-improve and continuous drain must not pick up tickets tagged to frozen subsystems, and backlog grooms should tag accordingly. Growth-rate control is a goal-level concern for a self-hosting 101k-line one-month-old codebase; without it the orthogonal mass regrows.
- **R7. External actions through the approval boundary** (slice 7 — rerun CI, rollback), once R2's read-only loop has run long enough to trust. The digest-approval contract in `action_approval.rs` is the gate; the design work is the action vocabulary and its blast-radius tiers.

## Review provenance

This report was drafted from four independent codebase surveys (rk architecture/feature inventory; rk failure-mode catalogue; imp-castle comparison; reactive-groundwork assessment), then subjected to an adversarial fact-check (all 13 claim groups confirmed against the tree; two soft spots corrected: onboarding is 7,561 lines, not ~8,000; the daemon *exposes* a version that nothing compares) and a strategy critique (15 findings). The critique materially changed it: the single-cause "deleted the mind" framing was demoted to the consequence-level claim it can support; the seam table was re-classed into defaults/bugs/structural; the recommendation ordering was inverted to run the cheap reactive slice early as a forcing function; the reconciler was cut down from a flagship component to an incrementally-grown one with a written contract; and the security prerequisites (diagnostician role, payload hygiene), identity-by-name remedy, operator-interaction decision, generator constraints, and per-delivery-mode done-binding were added.
